## 3. Adoption signals — survey data

### WP Engine, "The State of Headless" (2021 → 2024 trend)
- **73%** of surveyed businesses (1,015 CTOs/CMOs/IT decision-makers, US/UK/Australia, avg. ~$800M revenue, fielded by Censuswide July 2024) now use headless architecture — up from **64%** in 2021 and a "nearly 40% increase from 2019." [wpengine.com, Sep 10 2024](https://wpengine.com/press-releases/state-of-headless-2024/)
- **98%** of non-adopters plan to evaluate headless solutions within 12 months. [same source]
- **80%** say headless gives them a competitive edge in delivering new digital experiences; **~70%** cite competitiveness as the top benefit. [same source]
- **65%** cite budget as a barrier to adoption despite the above. [same source]
- CMOs are *more* involved than CTOs in headless decisions (63% vs 52% "heavily involved"). [same source]

### Storyblok, "The State of CMS 2025" (May 2025, 1,300–1,719 CMS users across 5 countries — figure varies by citing source)
- **61%** of teams run 2+ CMSs simultaneously; reasons: minimize delivery risk (61%), POC with new CMS (50%), new tech stack requirement (47%), omnichannel support (44%). [storyblok.com, Mar 7 2025](https://www.storyblok.com/mp/cms-statistics)
- Headless-adoption benefits reported: 69% improved time-to-market/productivity, 58% better site performance, 57% better personalization/UX, 50% stronger security, 41% measurable ROI increase. [same source]
- **44%** say their current CMS lacks built-in AI content tools. [same source]
- **65%** say visual editing + developer independence are now "essential." [same source]
- WordPress still holds **34%** CMS market share; 49% of its users say publishing takes over an hour, 14% say a full day+. [same source]
- Regional data points: 86% of Dutch headless users reported increased ROI; 77% of Dutch companies plan to migrate CMS soon; 70% of German companies saw performance/scaling gains post-migration. [same source]
- Cited by Mordor Intelligence (sourcing the same Storyblok report, 1,300 respondents): planned growth in AR/VR and voice-speaker content delivery channels at 15%, smartwatches at 14%. [mordorintelligence.com](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/headless-cms-software-market)
- **Talent premium:** headless CMS skills carry a **29% salary premium for developers** and **85% for marketers** vs. legacy-CMS skills in the US (Storyblok, May 2025, as cited by Mordor Intelligence). [mordorintelligence.com](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/headless-cms-software-market)

### Storyblok / Sago-IVP, developer sentiment survey (200 senior developers at medium-large businesses, reported June 2025)
- **47.5%** of developers say their tech stack made them consider quitting in the past year; **31%** think about quitting regularly (past month). [digit.fyi, Jun 19 2025](https://www.digit.fyi/developers-are-embarrassed-over-outdated-tech-stacks/)
- Top day-to-day pain: fixing bugs / maintaining legacy systems (27.5%), stakeholders not understanding tech limits (21.5%), unclear/shifting requirements (14%). [same source]
- Tech-stack frustrations: lack of key functionality (51%), difficulty maintaining it (47%), lack of AI compatibility (31%). [same source]
- **Embarrassing** aspects of current stack: legacy systems (24.5%), technical debt/workarounds (22.5%), outdated frameworks/libraries (18%); only 14% report no embarrassment. [same source]
- **67.5%** say their company's CMS specifically holds them back from doing their best work; 29.5% would replace it with one offering a better developer experience; 23.5% want modern tech-stack integration; only **4%** call their CMS "perfect." [same source]
- **89.5%** of these developers use AI to code "frequently or constantly." [same source]

### MACH Alliance, 2025 Global Annual Research (561 IT decision-makers, director+, companies with 5,000+ employees / $500M+ revenue, US/UK/DE/FR/CA/AU; fielded Nov 2024, published Jan 2025)
- **87%** of member/respondent organizations have "widely implemented" MACH (Microservices, API-first, Cloud-native, Headless) technologies. [businesswire.com, Jan 14 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250114451879/en/MACH-Alliance-Research-Reinforces-Growing-Appetite-for-Composable-Technology-Finds-7-Increase-in-Companies-Proving-ROI)
- **9 in 10** (90%) of MACH adopters say it met or exceeded ROI expectations — a 7-point YoY increase. [same source]
- Top cited benefits: improved customer experience (55%), greater automation (54%), greater organizational agility (50%). [same source]

### Migration cost/friction benchmark (for objection-handling slide)
- Sanity's 2025 enterprise migration guidance: a standard move from monolithic CMS to full headless setup typically takes **16–24 weeks** for core content-model configuration + integration wiring (cited by Mordor Intelligence). [mordorintelligence.com](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/headless-cms-software-market)

*Implication for VexCMS:* the adoption data is unambiguous — headless is mainstream and growing, developer dissatisfaction with legacy/monolithic CMS is severe (near 50% "considered quitting"), and the #1 complaint is poor developer experience, not missing marketer features. This squarely matches VexCMS's "code-first, type-safe, real-time by default" positioning. The 29–85% salary premium for headless CMS skills signals genuine scarcity VexCMS's Payload-familiar DX could lower.

### Worked example (top-down), with assumptions flagged

1. Start from the **headless CMS software market**: $1.8B in 2025 [Grand View, §1].
2. `[ASSUMPTION]` Restrict to the sub-segment of that market built by developers on **TypeScript/Next.js/React stacks** (VexCMS's natural buyer, vs. PHP/Vue/other-stack headless users). Using Next.js's 3.5% share of all websites [W3Techs, §4] and React's broader 6.1% share [W3Techs, §4] as rough stand-ins for "JS/TS-native web dev," `[ASSUMPTION: apply ~15–25% as a plausible TS/Next.js share of headless-CMS buyers]` → **SAM ≈ $270M–$450M** (15–25% of $1.8B).
3. `[ASSUMPTION]` Further restrict to companies that would specifically value a **Convex-native** CMS (i.e., already on or willing to adopt Convex as backend) rather than any TS backend. Convex's ecosystem is currently ~10,000 paying teams / ~500K developers [news.convex.dev, PRNewswire, §4/§6] versus Next.js's much larger installed base (67M weekly npm downloads). `[ASSUMPTION: treat Convex's ~10,000 paying teams as a rough ceiling for "will pay for a Convex-native enterprise CMS add-on in the near term"]` → **SOM (near-term, beachhead) ≈ low hundreds of paying teams**, i.e., a few percent of Convex's current paying-team base, consistent with being an early entrant in a brand-new sub-category.

### Worked example (bottom-up), with assumptions flagged

1. Take Convex's disclosed **~10,000 paying teams** [news.convex.dev, §6] as the pool of organizations already paying for Convex and therefore closest to adopting a Convex-native CMS without a backend-migration cost.
2. Apply WP Engine's finding that **73%** of comparable (CTO/CMO-led, revenue-generating) organizations already use or are evaluating headless CMS [WP Engine, §3] → `[ASSUMPTION: apply the 73% headless-adoption rate to the Convex paying-team population]` ≈ **7,300 Convex teams** plausibly in-market for *some* headless CMS.
3. `[ASSUMPTION]` Apply VexCMS's planned flat annual per-company enterprise add-on license (content branching, SSO/SAML, approval workflows, audit log, localization) at an illustrative price point of **$2,000–$10,000/year** (bracketing Convex's own disclosed Business-plan minimum of **$2,500/month = $30,000/year** [news.convex.dev/enterprise-launch, Apr 2026] as an upper-bound sanity check for what Convex-ecosystem buyers already pay for infrastructure add-ons) → **SOM (enterprise add-on ARR), if VexCMS converts `[ASSUMPTION: 5–10%]` of the 7,300 in-market Convex teams at $2–10K/year ≈ $0.7M–$7.3M ARR.**

**Both approaches land in the same order of magnitude** — a realistic initial beachhead SOM in the **low-to-mid single-digit millions of ARR**, growing with (a) Convex's own ecosystem growth (10x in 9 months per Convex's own messaging, §6) and (b) the broader headless-CMS category CAGR of ~18–20% [§1]. Neither the TAM ($1.5–4B headless CMS, or $4.7–5.2B BaaS) nor the SAM is a useful standalone number for VexCMS's pitch — the credible near-term story is **"ride Convex's ecosystem growth + the TypeScript/AI-agent tailwind into a beachhead inside an already-paying, already-TypeScript-native developer base,"** not "capture X% of a multi-billion-dollar headless CMS TAM."
